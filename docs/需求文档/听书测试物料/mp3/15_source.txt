The house wasn't just quiet; it was a tomb.

I unlocked the front door, the heavy oak swinging open on silent, well-oiled hinges.

"Aurora?"

My voice died in the cavernous foyer. No answer.

I glanced at my watch. It had been three hours since the Gala. Three hours since Ilene had collapsed on the sidewalk with her convenient "heart attack." The doctors at the ER had called it a panic attack. Of course they did.

I had sent a car back to pick Aurora up from the roadside where she'd stormed off, but the driver reported he couldn't find her.

She was probably sulking in the guest room. Or maybe, just maybe, she had finally listened to me and gone up to the penthouse.

I climbed the stairs, yanking my tie loose. The silk felt like a noose.

I was tired. I was so goddamn tired of refereeing this endless war between the two of them.

I pushed open the door to our master bedroom.

Empty.

The bed was made, pristine and undisturbed. Her vanity was clear.

I frowned, a prickle of unease touching the back of my neck.

I strode to the closet.

Her clothes were there. Her shoes were lined up in rows.

But something was wrong.

The air felt... sterile. Devoid of her scent. Abandoned.

I turned to the nightstand.

Her wedding ring sat on the dark mahogany surface, catching the dim light.

Next to it lay the black fountain pen I had given her at the restaurant earlier that evening.

And a note.

It wasn't in an envelope. It was just a piece of hotel stationery, folded once, sharp and final.

I picked it up. My hands-hands that had dealt violence without hesitation, hands that controlled this city-were trembling.

I unfolded the paper.

You chose.

Two words. A verdict.

Panic, cold and serrated, pierced my chest.

"Aurora!" I roared, the sound tearing from my throat.

I sprinted to the security room, slamming the door open.

"Where is she?" I barked at the guard on duty.

He spun around, his face draining of color. "Sir, we... we can't reach her. The tracker in her phone is offline."

"Offline? What do you mean offline?"

"We found the device in a trash can outside the Pierre Hotel, Sir. She must have dumped it immediately after the Gala."

"Then find her!" I slammed my fist on the console. "Pull the city surveillance. Retrace her steps from the hotel."

The guard's fingers flew across the keyboard. "We did, Sir. We tracked a vehicle leaving the sector near the I-95 on-ramp."

"Show me."

"Sir..." The guard hesitated, looking sick. "You need to see this."

He handed me a tablet. I grabbed it, my grip threatening to crack the screen.

I checked the logs first. One missed call on my secure line. 10:42 PM.

I froze.

I remembered that call. I was in the ambulance, holding Ilene's hand while she wept, begging me not to leave her. My earpiece had buzzed. I saw the "Priority Red" code.

I had thought it was a glitch. Or Aurora calling to scream at me again.

I had declined it.

I looked at the timestamp on the video file. 10:44 PM.

Two minutes after I ignored her.

"Play it," I ordered, my voice sounding like it belonged to a stranger.

A grainy video feed popped up on the monitors.

A black van was speeding onto the highway merge. Suddenly, the back door flew open.

A body tumbled out.

I stopped breathing.

I watched my wife hit the asphalt. I watched her roll, limbs flailing, the violence of the impact jarring even through the screen. I watched her lie still.

And then, I watched a gray sedan pull up two minutes later. A man got out. He picked her up-she was limp, broken-and placed her carefully into his car.

I didn't recognize the car.

But I recognized the way my heart stopped beating.

She didn't just leave.

She jumped.

She had thrown herself out of a moving vehicle to get away from me.

I sank into the chair, the silence of the house roaring in my ears like a judgment.

I was the Underboss. I controlled the streets. I controlled the flow of money and blood in this city.

But staring at that empty screen, I realized I controlled nothing.

I had protected the wrong woman.

And the right one was gone.
