I sat in the security room until the blue glare of the monitors seared into my retinas.

The loop played over and over.

The door opening. The jump. The roll. The gray sedan speeding into the night.

She was gone.

The silence in the house wasn't peaceful; it was oppressive. It pressed against my eardrums like deep water, threatening to crush me.

I stood up. My legs felt stiff, heavy as lead, like I had aged twenty years in twenty minutes.

I needed to see it. I needed to see the physical proof that she hadn't just left for the night. I needed to see the void she left behind.

I forced myself down the hallway to the east wing. This was her sanctuary. Her art studio. The only room in the estate where Ilene wasn't allowed-the only place that was truly Aurora's.

I pushed the door open.

I expected the earthy smell of turpentine and oil paint. I expected the chaos of canvases stacked against the walls, the splatter of color on the floor that she refused to clean because she said it was part of the process.

Instead, I was met with the chemical sting of bleach.

The room was white.

Stark, blinding, clinical white.

The canvases were gone. The easels were dismantled and stacked in the corner like firewood. The floor had been scrubbed raw.

There was nothing here.

It looked like a room waiting for a tenant. Not a room where my wife had spent five years pouring her soul onto fabric.

She hadn't just left, Ethan. She had erased herself.

I walked to the center of the room. My footsteps echoed, sharp and lonely against the bare walls.

She had planned this. You don't exorcise a room like this in an hour. She had been packing, scrubbing, and erasing while I was busy holding Ilene's hand.

A soft scuffle behind me made me turn.

It was Maria, the head housekeeper. She stood in the doorway, twisting her apron in her hands until her knuckles turned white. Her eyes were red and swollen.

"Mr. Bruce," she whispered.

"Where is it?" I asked. My voice sounded like gravel grinding together. "Where is her art?"

"She burned it, Sir."

I froze. "What?"

"Two days ago. In the incinerator out back. She said..." Maria choked on a sob. "She said that trash doesn't belong in a palace."

My chest constricted. That was something Ilene had said to her at dinner last week.

"Why didn't you stop her?" I snapped, the anger flaring hot and fast.

"I tried. She told me it was an order." Maria looked up at me, and for the first time in ten years, I saw open judgment in her eyes. "She came back from the Gala tonight, Sir. She came back to get her bag."

"You saw her?"

"Yes. She was crying. She didn't have her crutches. She was limping so bad I thought she would fall."

"Why didn't you call me?"

"I tried to help her," Maria said, her voice gaining a sudden, sharp edge. "I asked her to wait for you. I told her you would fix it."

I took a threatening step toward her. "And?"

"She laughed, Sir. It was a terrible, broken sound. She said there was nothing to fix. She said she saw you."

I stopped dead.

"Saw me what?"

"She said she saw you kiss Miss Wolf. She said you looked happy."

The air left my lungs as if I'd been gutted.

I didn't kiss Ilene because I wanted to. I kissed her to shut her up. To stop the scene. To protect the Family's image in front of the Commission. It was a tactic. A strategy.

But Aurora didn't see a strategy.

She saw her husband choosing his mistress.

"Get out," I whispered.

Maria didn't move. "She left her wedding ring, Mr. Bruce. She said it was too heavy to carry anymore."

"Get out!" I roared.

Maria flinched and hurried away, leaving the echo of my shout to bounce off the empty walls.

I was alone in the white room.

I looked at the sterile walls.

I had built a fortress to keep her safe. I had killed men who looked at her wrong. I was the Underboss. I was the monster in the dark who protected the innocent.

But standing in the bleach-scented void of her studio, I realized the truth.

I wasn't the protector.

I was the reason she needed protection.
