Ethan Bruce POV

A week of silence passed. Seven days of dead air.

The city wasn't just quiet; it was holding its breath.

The rumors had already begun to rot the foundation of our reputation. The other families were whispering in the dark corners of their clubs. The Underboss lost his wife. The Bruce family is bleeding. They are weak.

I didn't give a damn about the rumors. The only thing that mattered was the silence on the other end of the phone.

I needed to break something.

I drove to the penthouse. Ilene's apartment. The golden cage I paid for. The place I visited to pay my debts in flesh and guilt.

I hadn't spoken to her since the hospital. I had sent doctors, nurses, and a rotation of guards, but I hadn't stepped foot inside myself. I couldn't look at her without seeing Aurora falling out of that van, like a broken doll discarded on the pavement.

But tonight, I needed noise. I needed to scream at someone who deserved it.

I didn't buzz up. I bypassed the doorman with the master key.

I took the private elevator to the top floor, watching the numbers climb like a countdown.

The doors slid open.

I heard music. Jazz. Smooth, expensive, and utterly discordant with the chaos in my mind.

And laughter.

Ilene's laugh. It wasn't the hysterical, gasping sob she used to manipulate me. It was a throat-deep, satisfied sound of victory.

"He's a wreck, Leo. It's pathetic."

I froze.

Leo.

Leo Vance. The Capo of the rival syndicate. The man whose soldiers had tried to run my trucks off the road last month.

"He's running around the city like a headless chicken," a male voice drawled, dripping with amusement. "My guys said he looks like he hasn't slept in a week."

"Good," Ilene said. I heard the distinct clink of crystal. "He deserves it. Treating me like a dirty secret for five years. Now that the bitch is gone, he'll come crawling back. He always does."

My hand didn't just hover over my gun; it itched to empty the magazine.

"You're sure she's gone?" Leo asked.

"Oh, she's gone. The kidnapping scared her straight. I told you it would work."

The world didn't just tilt; the ground vanished beneath my feet.

I stepped out of the elevator.

They were in the living room, a tableau of betrayal. Leo Vance was lounging on my leather sofa, his boots resting on my coffee table. Ilene was straddling his lap, a glass of wine in her hand, celebrating the destruction of my life.

They froze when they saw me.

Ilene dropped the glass. It shattered on the floor, red wine splashing across the hardwood like a fresh arterial spray.

"Ethan," she breathed. Her face went ashen.

Leo went for his gun.

He was fast.

But I was possessed.

I didn't draw my weapon. I crossed the room in two predatory strides.

I snatched the heavy wine bottle from the table and swung it in a violent arc, smashing it directly across Leo's face.

He screamed.

The sound was music-a symphony of pain.

He fell back onto the sofa, blood pouring from his nose and forehead, blinding him. Ilene scrambled off him, backing away until she hit the wall, her hands raised in surrender.

"Ethan, wait! I can explain!"

I ignored her.

I grabbed Leo by the throat and hauled him up. He was a big man, but fear makes even the heaviest men light.

"The kidnapping," I said. My voice was calm. Terrifyingly, unnaturally calm.

"She paid me! She bankrolled the whole thing! She said you wouldn't pick up the phone!"

I tightened my grip, feeling his windpipe strain against my fingers.

"And the van?" I asked. "The men who took her?"

"My guys," Leo wheezed, his eyes bulging. "Just a scare tactic. We weren't gonna kill her. Just... rough her up. Make you look bad."

I looked at Ilene.

She was trembling against the wall, a portrait of guilt.

"You set it up," I said.

"I did it for us!" she screamed, her voice cracking. "She was in the way, Ethan! She was poisoning you against me!"

"She called me," I said, the memory cutting through me like a knife. "When she was in that van. She called me on the red line."

Ilene's eyes widened in horror.

"And I declined it," I whispered. "Because you were holding my hand."

The rage that exploded in my chest wasn't hot. It was cold. Absolute zero.

I had let my wife be dragged off the street by my enemy because I was busy comforting the woman who paid him to do it.

I looked back at Leo.

"You touched her," I said.

"I didn't," Leo choked out, clawing at my wrist. "My guys... they just grabbed her."

"Someone twisted her arm. Someone broke her ribs."

"Ethan, please-"

I threw him onto the floor with enough force to crack the floorboards.

"You broke my property," I said.

I unbuttoned my cuffs, rolling up my sleeves with methodical precision.

"Now I break you."
