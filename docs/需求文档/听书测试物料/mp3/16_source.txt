I tore the bedroom apart.

I didn't know what I was searching for. Maybe a sign that she was coming back. Maybe a ticket stub. Maybe just a mistake.

I ripped the drawers out of her dresser and overturned them onto the floor.

Empty.

No clothes. No jewelry. Even the silk scarves I had bought her in Milan were gone.

She had taken everything that was hers. And she had left everything that was mine.

I stalked to the nightstand.

The ring sat there. A five-carat diamond that I had slid onto her finger with a promise to cherish her.

Now, it looked like nothing more than a shard of glass. Cold. Dead.

Next to it was the paper.

I picked it up again.

You chose.

Two words.

No anger. No accusations. Just a fact.

I crumpled the paper in my fist.

"I didn't choose her!" I roared at the silence.

"I chose duty! I chose honor!"

My voice echoed against the bare walls, mocking me.

But the truth festered in my gut. Duty to a woman who had manipulated me. Honor to a ghost of a man who died ten years ago.

And in exchange, I had sacrificed the woman who washed the blood off my hands every night.

I grabbed my phone.

"Capo," I snarled the second the line connected.

"Boss?"

"Wake them up. Everyone. I want every soldier on the pavement. I want eyes on every train station, airport, and bus terminal. Nothing moves without us knowing."

"On it."

I hung up.

Nothing.

The art curator. The civilian. The man she smiled at when she thought I wasn't looking.
